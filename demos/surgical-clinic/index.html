Costs, insurance and recovery

Straight answers to what people ask us most. Anything else, ask the desk.

Ask on WhatsApp Call the desk
How much does surgery cost?

It depends on the procedure, the technique (laparoscopic, laser or open), the hospital room you choose and your general health. We don't quote a fixed price before examining you, because it would not be accurate.

After the consultation you get a written, itemised estimate covering surgeon, anaesthesia, hospital stay and medicines. The consultation fee is ₹[XXX].

Can I get cashless treatment with my insurance?

Yes, through our insurer and TPA tie-ups. Bring your policy number or e-card and a photo ID to the consultation. Our desk checks your cover and sends the pre-authorisation request; approval usually comes within a day.

Some costs may not be covered, such as room rent above your policy limit, consumables, or a co-payment. We tell you these before admission. If your insurer isn't on our list, you can pay and claim reimbursement; we give you the documents.

Do you offer EMI?

Yes. You can pay in monthly instalments through our partner finance providers, subject to their approval and documents. Tenure and interest depend on the provider; the desk will show you the current options.

How long will recovery take?

After laparoscopic surgery many people return to desk work in one to two weeks. Hernia repair usually means no heavy lifting for four to six weeks. Day-care procedures for piles or fistula often need three days to two weeks, depending on the technique.

These are typical ranges. Your age, health, the condition and the type of work you do all matter, so your surgeon will give you a plan for your case.

How should I prepare for my consultation?
  • Bring earlier reports, scans and prescriptions.
  • Write down the medicines you take, including blood thinners and diabetes medicines.
  • Bring your insurance card or e-card and a photo ID.
  • Come 10 minutes early to register. A family member is welcome to join.
How should I prepare for surgery?
  • Don't eat or drink for the hours your surgeon tells you, usually six to eight hours before.
  • Stop or continue medicines only as advised. Never stop blood thinners on your own.
  • Wear loose, comfortable clothes and leave jewellery at home.
  • Arrange for someone to take you home and stay with you the first night.
What happens to the details I share?

We use your name, mobile number, preferred date and procedure of interest only to contact you about an appointment. We don't sell or share them for marketing. Read the full privacy notice.
